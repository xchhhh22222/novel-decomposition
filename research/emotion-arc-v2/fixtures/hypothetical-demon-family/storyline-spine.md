# Existing Story Spine retained

The emotional architecture supplies why the reader cares and what must be visibly paid. The action layer still uses the existing nine-stage plotline lifecycle.

| Stage | Macro A — home protection | Macro B — protection-order inquiry |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trigger | Warning allocation is diverted; an attack reaches the home perimeter. | A rescue record reveals a modified authorization trail. |
| Goal | Get family into a defensible state and restore real protection. | Prove the beneficiary chain and change an operative protection rule. |
| Resistance | Failed ward, divided family choices, scarce rescue resource, active attackers. | Regulator suspicion, sealed logs, beneficiary influence, protagonist's exposed power. |
| Nodes | Verify threat → obtain access → create route → defend/evacuate → restore barrier. | Preserve evidence → identify authority path → force disclosure → public/official contest. |
| Choice | Use a restricted ability and accept an identity cost. | Risk personal exoneration evidence to expose the wider allocation scheme. |
| Cost | Recovery limit, resource debt, and surveillance. | Lost patronage, restricted permissions, and greater family visibility. |
| Payoff | Family agency matters; immediate threat ends; protection actually works. | Beneficiary chain is proven and one rule/resource flow changes. |
| State change | Home is safer, protagonist is exposed, authorization evidence exists. | Protagonist has a new public/institutional role and stronger opponents. |
| Next entry | Evidence plus exposure starts B before A's final settlement scene. | A new dispute over who controls the redesigned protection resource. |

No chapter text or fixed chapter count is generated. Climax backplanning and `architecture_300` remain downstream, unchanged processes.
